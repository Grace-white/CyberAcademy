Introduction to Networking

Welcome to the first module of Networking Fundamentals. In this lesson, you will learn what computer networks are, how devices communicate, and the basic components that make network communication possible.

Lesson 1: What Is a Computer Network?

A computer network is a group of connected devices that communicate with one another and share information, resources, and services.

Networks can connect computers, phones, servers, printers, routers, switches, and many other devices.

Lesson 2: Why Networks Matter

Networks allow people and organizations to communicate, access information, share files, use online services, and manage systems from different locations.

Understanding networking is especially important in cybersecurity because security professionals need to understand how data moves between systems.

Lesson 3: Basic Network Components

Computer

A device that can send, receive, process, or store information on a network.

Switch

A device that connects devices within a local network and forwards network traffic to the appropriate device.

Router

A device that connects different networks and helps determine where network traffic should be sent.

Server

A computer or system that provides services or resources to other devices on a network.

Lesson 4: Local and Wide Area Networks

A Local Area Network, or LAN, connects devices within a limited area such as a home, school, office, or laboratory.

A Wide Area Network, or WAN, connects networks across larger geographical areas. The Internet is the largest example of an interconnected global network.

Key Takeaways

  • A network connects devices so they can communicate.
  • Switches connect devices within a local network.
  • Routers connect different networks.
  • Servers provide services and resources.
  • Networking knowledge is fundamental to cybersecurity.
