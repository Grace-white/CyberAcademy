Introduction to Ethical Hacking

Welcome to the first module of the Ethical Hacking course. This lesson introduces ethical hacking, cybersecurity roles, security principles, and responsible security testing.

Lesson 1: What Is Ethical Hacking?

Ethical hacking is the authorized process of examining systems, applications, and networks to identify security weaknesses and help organizations improve their security.

Ethical hackers work with permission and follow defined rules, objectives, and boundaries.

Lesson 2: Ethical Hackers vs Malicious Attackers

Ethical Hacker

An ethical hacker has permission to test a system and reports security weaknesses responsibly.

Malicious Attacker

A malicious attacker attempts to access, damage, disrupt, or misuse systems without authorization.

Lesson 3: The Importance of Authorization

Authorization is one of the most important principles of ethical security testing.

Before testing a system, security professionals should understand what they are allowed to test, when testing may occur, which techniques are permitted, and what systems are included in the engagement.

Lesson 4: Scope and Rules of Engagement

A security assessment should have a clearly defined scope. The scope identifies the systems, applications, networks, and activities that are included in the assessment.

Scope

Defines what systems or resources may be tested.

Rules of Engagement

Define how, when, and under what conditions testing should be performed.

Lesson 5: Core Security Principles

Ethical hacking is closely connected to the fundamental principles of cybersecurity.

  • Confidentiality — protecting information from unauthorized access.
  • Integrity — protecting information from unauthorized modification.
  • Availability — ensuring systems and services remain accessible when needed.

Lesson 6: The Ethical Hacking Process

Security assessments generally follow a structured process. The exact process varies depending on the organization, scope, and objectives of the assessment.

  1. Planning and authorization
  2. Information gathering
  3. Security assessment
  4. Validation of findings
  5. Documentation and reporting
  6. Remediation and follow-up

Lesson 7: Safe Learning Environment

Ethical hacking skills should be practiced only in systems you own or in environments where you have explicit authorization.

Cyber Academy labs should provide controlled environments where students can learn security concepts safely without affecting real systems or people.

Key Takeaways

  • Ethical hacking is authorized security testing.
  • Permission and scope must be established before testing.
  • Ethical hackers identify weaknesses and help improve security.
  • Confidentiality, integrity, and availability are core security principles.
  • Security assessments should follow a structured process.
  • Cybersecurity techniques should be practiced in authorized environments.
